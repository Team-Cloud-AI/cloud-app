# First Place Visit Notes

First Place challenge partner notes

Response from challenge partner Josh-

Thanks for sharing your team's idea! It was great to meet your teammate yesterday. Glad the tour of First Place–Phoenix is already sparking ideas for your team. A few things I'd encourage your team to consider as you develop a concept:
What will motivate someone to use the tool? It's one thing to build a functional tool, but another to create something someone wants to engage with consistently. Some individuals may be motivated by seeing their progress, earning rewards, or reaching financial goals. Others may not immediately connect how saving money, developing skills, or making everyday decisions contributes to their independence.
How might the tool reinforce positive behaviors and make those connections meaningful? I like the idea of gamification, but would encourage you to think about what makes those rewards personally motivating. Not everyone will respond to the same incentives.
What is the primary problem you're trying to solve? Your concept includes budgeting, spending protections, predictive AI, investing, skill tracking, and rewards. There are some interesting possibilities here, but that's also a lot for one solution to accomplish.
I'd encourage your team to identify the one or two features that would make the greatest difference for your intended users. What is the core experience you want to demonstrate? You can certainly envision additional features down the road, but a focused solution that works well may be more compelling than one trying to accomplish everything at once.
Who could benefit from this beyond First Place–Phoenix? As I shared with the hackers who toured, I'd encourage you to think about the broader community of neurodivergent adults. First Place–Phoenix can be a great place to learn about challenges and test ideas, but your solution doesn't necessarily need to be designed exclusively for our residents.
Could this be useful for someone living with family, independently in the community, or receiving other forms of support? Thinking about accessibility, affordability, and scalability early on could help strengthen your concept.
How much independence and choice does the user retain? I was particularly interested in your idea of a card that helps someone manage spending limits and upcoming financial responsibilities. Consider how the tool could support decision-making rather than simply restrict purchases.
For example, could it help someone understand the consequences of a purchase, recognize an upcoming bill, or work toward a savings goal? Ideally, the technology builds financial confidence and decision-making skills over time, rather than creating dependence on the technology itself.
What is realistic for the hackathon? I'd love to see your team identify a practical use case and demonstrate how AI adds value to that experience. You don't necessarily need to build a functioning financial card or investment platform to show how your concept could work. A prototype demonstrating a personalized budgeting experience, meaningful feedback, and progress toward an individual goal could be a great starting point.
What's already available, and what makes your solution different? I encourage teams to apply this question to any innovation: "What does the marketplace already offer, and what is your unique value proposition?"
There are already financial tools and cards that help individuals monitor spending, set limits, and manage budgets. Similarly, there's no shortage of apps designed to support executive functioning, daily routines, and skill development. Explore what's already out there before building something from scratch. What do existing solutions do well? Where do they fall short, particularly for adults with autism and/or I/DD? Is there a gap in accessibility, personalization, motivation, or how these tools connect everyday decisions to greater independence?
You don't necessarily need to create something entirely new. Sometimes the strongest innovation comes from recognizing what's missing in an existing solution and finding a better way to address it.
**The question I'd leave your team with is: Why would someone choose your solution over what's already available, and what meaningful difference would it make in their life?
There's a lot of potential in your concept; keep exploring the bigger vision while identifying a clear, achievable starting point that demonstrates how the technology could meaningfully support someone's independence.


First Place – Challenge Partner Notes

Below are some of the recurring challenges and concerns discussed by First Place, along with how frequently they occur and the problem behind it.

  1. Cooking Skills and Independence
Frequency: Daily

First Place provides individuals with access to kitchens, cookware, and appliances to help them develop independent cooking skills. The goal is to teach individuals how to prepare their own meals so they feel confident using a stove and other kitchen appliances when they eventually live independently.
However, one recurring concern is that many individuals have been relying heavily on convenient food options, such as chicken tenders and other easy-to-prepare meals, rather than taking advantage of these resources and developing their cooking abilities.

  3. Stove Safety and Cooking Accessibility
Frequency: Weekly (estimated)

Another challenge involves the stove shut-off device. The current system can be restrictive, particularly for individuals who enjoy cooking meals that require longer preparation times.
There was also discussion about the possibility of developing a controller that would allow the stove's on-and-off functions to be managed more easily. This could provide individuals with greater flexibility and control over their cooking experience while still maintaining appropriate safety measures.
 
  4. Family Connections and Separation Anxiety
Frequency: Weekly (estimated)

Another recurring concern is the difficulty some individuals experience when they are away from their families. Being in a new environment without familiar companions or loved ones can create feelings of anxiety, isolation, and discomfort.
This highlights the importance of finding ways to help individuals feel more connected to their families while gradually building confidence, comfort, and independence in their living environment.
 
  5. Transportation Accessibility and Costs
Frequency: Weekly (estimated)

Transportation has also been identified as a significant challenge. The high cost of transportation services, combined with long wait times and slow service, can make it difficult for individuals to get where they need to go.
Improving transportation accessibility, affordability, and reliability could make a meaningful difference in helping individuals participate in daily activities and maintain greater independence.
 
  6. Tracking Personal Development and Skill Progress
Frequency: Daily

One of the biggest opportunities identified is creating a better way to track individual progress and personal development.
It can be difficult to consistently measure the skills individuals are learning and improving over time. This includes practical life skills such as financial management, cooking, budgeting, and other abilities that contribute to independent living.A
A personalized progress-tracking system could help individuals, families, and staff monitor skill development, recognize achievements, identify areas that need improvement, and better understand how much progress each individual is making.

Overall Opportunity
Develop tools and solutions that promote greater independence, improve everyday accessibility, and help neurodivergent individuals build confidence in their abilities. The goal is not simply to assist, but to empower individuals with the skills, resources, and support they need to live more independently.

Most Frequently Occurring Challenges:

Cooking skills and independence. 

Tracking personal development and skill progress.

These two areas appear to present the greatest opportunities for improvement based on the recurring challenges discussed.
